As a Business Analyst working in an Agile environment, the user story is your primary currency. It's the artifact that connects the business need with the technical execution. However, writing a *good* user story is harder than it looks.

## The Problem with Vague Requirements

Consider a story like: *"As a user, I want a dashboard so I can see data."*
This is practically useless for a development team. What data? What kind of user? What decisions are they trying to make?

## The INVEST Criteria

A universally accepted framework for writing effective user stories is the **INVEST** acronym:
*   **Independent**: The story should stand alone and not depend heavily on other stories.
*   **Negotiable**: It's not a rigid contract; it's a starting point for conversation.
*   **Valuable**: It must deliver clear value to the end-user or the business.
*   **Estimable**: The team should have enough information to estimate its complexity.
*   **Small**: It should be small enough to complete within a single sprint.
*   **Testable**: There must be clear acceptance criteria to know when it's "done."

## Acceptance Criteria: The Real Meat

The acceptance criteria (often written in Given/When/Then format) are where the actual requirements live. They define the boundaries of the story and provide the QA team with exactly what they need to test. A BA who masters acceptance criteria is invaluable to a sprint team.
